02

Your role

White Glove Talent Advisor

You're a White Glove Talent Advisor. Sagan runs four revenue lines. White Glove is the premium one. Members pay a percentage of the first-year salary (25% retained, 30%, or 35% success-based) for us to find hires they can't find on their own. The playbook is different from the Passport/Core motion the rest of the recruiting team runs. Fewer searches, more depth, higher stakes.

The job, in one line: you own the search end-to-end, from kickoff to placement to the 90-day check-in. The hiring manager is your counterpart. You are their recruiter, not a coordinator handing off to a sourcing team.

Concretely, that means:

  • Running the kickoff call yourself. Coming out with a real profile, not a wish list.
  • Owning the sourcing plan. You lean on the recruiting team when it helps, but the strategy is yours.
  • Screening every candidate before the member sees them. If a bad candidate lands on the shortlist, that's your call, not the sourcer's.
  • Building a shortlist inside 7 to 14 days from kickoff. That's the White Glove promise.
  • Managing the interview process on both sides. Prep, debrief, keep the candidate warm.
  • Handling the offer conversation. Reading resistance. Closing when it's time.
  • Staying in the room 30, 60, 90 days after placement. Replacements happen in this window if they happen.
  • Feeding intel back to the AM team when you spot an expansion signal or a referral opportunity.

You will not walk in fluent. Nobody does. Migue will run your first two or three searches with you in the seat.

The workflow

Four steps. Simple, straightforward, clean.

Every White Glove engagement runs through the same four steps. Learn the shape by heart. Everything else, the calls, the shortlists, the offers, hangs off this.

Step 01
Sales closes the deal
  • Not your seat. Sales closes with one of three structures.
  • $500 deposit + 35% of annual salary at placement
  • $1,000 deposit + 30% of annual salary at placement
  • No deposit, 25% retained paid in advance regardless of final salary
  • By the time you see it, the structure is locked.
Step 02
Kickoff
  • Your seat starts here.
  • Meet the hiring manager. Talk the role in real detail.
  • Build the relationship early. It carries the whole search.
  • Listen for upsell signals: other roles, other functions, adjacent needs.
  • Pull the intel that will sharpen your sourcing strategy.
Step 03
Find and present
  • Sourcing plan built off the kickoff intel.
  • Screen every candidate yourself before they hit the shortlist.
  • Shortlist inside 7 to 14 days.
  • Present properly: dossier, video intro, your recommendation.
Step 04
Close the deal
  • Manage the interview loop on both sides.
  • Handle the offer conversation. Read the resistance.
  • Land the placement.
  • Handoff to the AM for the 90-day plan and long-term relationship.

The mindset

Three things that separate White Glove from everything else

Members pay us premium fees because they can't get this hire anywhere else. Every search is a test of whether that's still true.

01
Speed is still the whole game.
Retained doesn't mean slow. Seven to fourteen days from kickoff to first shortlist. If you take three weeks, you've already lost the trust the member paid for.
02
Every shortlist is your reputation.
If the member rejects the whole shortlist, that's on you, not the sourcer. Read the brief three times. Screen every candidate. Never send filler to hit a number.
03
The hiring manager is buying the partnership, not the paperwork.
They pay a premium because they get a recruiter who actually thinks. Push back when the brief is wrong. Recommend candidates they wouldn't have shortlisted themselves. Be direct about market rates. That's the product.

The pricing you can offer

Three options. No freelancing.

Three pricing options. These are the only three. Do not invent new ones on a call. If a member wants something outside this, escalate to Jesus.

Structure How it works
35% success $500 non-refundable deposit, applied to the success fee. Standard success-based search. Best for members testing the model.
30% success $1,000 non-refundable deposit, applied to the success fee. Larger up-front commitment, better rate. Good for members serious about closing.
25% retained No deposit. 25% of annual salary paid in advance, regardless of final salary. Best rate. Retained, not success-based. For hard-to-fill senior and executive roles.

Learn to read which one the member should choose. Retained is the highest-signal option for a serious role. Success is the entry point. The 30% middle option is where most members land after they see one delivery.

Your compensation

Base salary plus White Glove bonus pool

White Glove has a shared bonus pool on top of base. It rewards the whole team when the team delivers. Here's how it works.

The pool

5% of total collected White Glove revenues each month. Calculated after any affiliate or lead-gen fees. Membership conversions count toward the pool for the full first year at $500/month net. Pool is paid once monthly revenue is reconciled and collections confirmed.

Your share, month by month

Share allocation vests from your start date. You build up to a full share at month 13. The pool is split proportionally each month by the shares in play across the team.

Tenure Your share
Months 1–30 (no share yet)
Months 4–60.25 shares
Months 7–90.50 shares
Months 10–120.75 shares
Month 13+1.00 full share

This structure rewards you for staying, delivering, and building a book of members who keep coming back. Migue is a full share. Guille is on the same vesting ramp you are, just further along. Ask either of them what a real month looks like.

The 12-month gameplan

From new TA to full share, running your own book
The path

To get there, you need to become the recruiter members ask for by name.

Katherine, the goal is not to just close reqs. The goal is that six months from now, a hiring manager finishes an interview with a candidate you sourced and says "who else is Katherine running for us?" That's what a White Glove Talent Advisor really is. Not a middleman. A partner the member trusts more than their internal team.

The best White Glove Talent Advisors do this
  • Read a brief and know what the member isn't saying
  • Deliver a shortlist that beats the member's own network
  • Push back on a bad requirement instead of nodding along
  • Close a candidate who has three competing offers
  • Know market rates cold. Never quote from memory. Always from data.
  • Stay in the room 30, 60, 90 days after the placement lands
Q1 · Months 1–3
Learn the playbook
Shadow Migue on live searches. Own one search from kickoff to shortlist by end of month 2. Deliver your first placement by end of month 3. Memorize pricing, benefits, and the replacement policy. By end of Q1 you should be able to run a kickoff call solo without a script.
Success looks like 1 placement delivered. 2 to 3 concurrent searches. Zero shortlist rejections you couldn't defend.
Q2 · Months 4–6
Build the muscle
Run 3 to 4 concurrent searches. Deliver 2 to 3 placements this quarter. Start handling candidate offer conversations solo. Hit the 7 to 14 day shortlist window consistently. Your first 0.25 share of the bonus pool kicks in month 4.
Success looks like 2 to 3 placements closed. Time-to-shortlist under 14 days. Bonus pool share vesting on schedule.
Q3 · Months 7–9
Run your own book
4 to 5 concurrent searches, all yours. Migue reviews, doesn't co-pilot. You handle escalations directly with the member. First repeat search from a member you placed for shows up. Share ramps to 0.50.
Success looks like 4+ placements in quarter. 1 repeat member. Members asking for you by name.
Q4 · Months 10–12
Full seat
Running a full book independently. Concurrent load of 5 to 6 active searches. Bringing in your own repeat business through member referrals and second-search asks. Share ramps to 0.75, then full share at month 13. You're a peer to Guille, not a junior.
Success looks like Consistent monthly placements. 2 to 3 members treating you as their primary recruiter. Full share on the pool from month 13.
